What is happening

    How the model works (agents, rules, measures)

    Each person has a true level of focus that drifts week to week around their own mean. The sensor reads focus plus noise; group B gets extra noise. The score is the reading normalised against a reference population (the “norm”) and squashed to 0–1. People near or below the cut-off feel pressure in proportion to the stakes. They respond with effort: part of it raises real focus, and the rest inflates only the signal (gaming). Audits reset gaming at random. Recalibration re-centres the norm on this week’s readings, including any gaming. Validity is the Pearson correlation between score and true focus across all 400 people. The half-life is the first week validity falls to half its lab value. Every run uses the same random seed, so the same settings give the same result.

    Your room. The share of Focus Game players who crossed the line in round 2 sets how fast agents adapt. The other settings describe a high-stakes institution: a cut-off with a consequence, little checking, recalibration every 8 weeks, and a small share of adaptation that is real effort, because in the game more taps never meant more focus.
